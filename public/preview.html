I noticed Virtual Networking is your weakest objective (52%). Want me to drill into VNet peering and UDRs, or jump to load balancing?
Explain when I need a UDR vs when system routes are enough.

Short answer: system routes cover the common cases. You add a UDR when you need to override them.

Azure automatically creates system routes for:

  • Traffic within a VNet (any subnet ↔ any subnet)
  • Traffic to peered VNets
  • Traffic to the internet (default 0.0.0.0/0)
  • Traffic to on-premises via VPN/ExpressRoute (when a gateway is connected)

You write a UDR when you must force traffic somewhere specific, most often:

  1. Forced tunnelling internet egress through Azure Firewall / an NVA (0.0.0.0/0 → 10.0.1.4)
  2. Hub-and-spoke spoke-to-spoke through a hub NVA (peering alone won't transit)
  3. Blackholing a specific prefix (next hop: None)

Exam trap: spoke-to-spoke through a hub firewall always needs UDRs on both spokes — VNet peering is non-transitive.
